Every photograph is assessed, then priced once together — roughly a second or two per angle.

How the estimate is built

  1. Segment the damage A YOLO11 segmentation model returns a pixel mask for each damaged region in every photograph, with its class and confidence.
  2. Measure the area Each mask is rasterised and its filled pixels counted, then expressed as a fraction of the vehicle area in that frame.
  3. Grade the severity The fraction is compared against two published thresholds per damage class to give minor, moderate or severe.
  4. Price once, across all angles Damage of the same type on the same part is merged before pricing, so a bumper photographed from two sides is bought once, not twice.

Severity is computed, not predicted. The training data carries no severity labels, so grading it with the network would mean inventing a ground truth. Instead the thresholds are documented and the measured area is shown against them on every result, so any verdict can be checked by hand.

Why more angles help. Area is measured against what is visible in the frame, so a dent filling a close-up grades higher than the same dent in a photograph of the whole car. Several angles give the estimate more than one measurement to work from, and each one is listed separately on the result so you can see which shot produced which figure.
